## 3. Architecture ledger

Each entry records a proposed terminal mechanism, its single decisive candidate lemma, the logical-strength test, scaling, a falsifier, and the old results it would use. A failed logical-strength test is a reason for rejection, not a qualification to be hidden in a theorem hypothesis. The ten entries change objects in substantially different ways; several are deliberately killed before elaboration.

### A. Minimal singular initial data and a compact critical orbit

**Mechanism.** Minimize the critical initial norm among singularity-generating data, obtain a compact orbit modulo translation/dilation, then apply rigidity. **Decisive candidate lemma.** Minimality of the initial norm forces forward orbit precompactness. **Logical-strength test.** This would be an actual structural lemma, rather than an endpoint estimate, but initial minimality supplies the opposite norm inequality from the one the shortcut needs. Rusin–Šverák compactify the set of minimizing *initial data*, not its evolving orbit [RS]. Section 4 gives an exact dynamical counterexample to that inference. **Scaling.** The homogeneous `H^(1/2)` norm is invariant under NS dilation. **Falsifier.** Time-translate a purported minimizer; it remains singularity-generating but need not remain minimizing. **Reuse/discard.** Reuse local theory and critical scaling; discard all quotient machinery. **Disposition.** Retire the automatic-orbit-compactness shortcut. Minimizing an orbit supremum instead does not supply a finite critical threshold absent an a priori bound.

### B. Ancient solutions with two retained time marks

**Mechanism.** Produce a bounded ancient mild solution which changes from a global half-amplitude bound to a unit-amplitude peak, and prove such amplification impossible. **Decisive candidate lemma.** No bounded three-dimensional ancient mild solution has the two marks in (5.3). **Logical-strength test.** This is a theorem about a prescribed ancient class, not a renamed endpoint norm. It does not by itself cover every finite-time singularity: Section 5 explicitly separates the slow-record regime. **Scaling.** Velocity is normalized by its record amplitude, length by its inverse, and time by its inverse square. **Falsifier.** Constants, accelerating spatially uniform non-mild solutions, and escape of all vorticity to infinity. Section 5 excludes these in the marked class. **Reuse/discard.** Reuse local mild theory, scaling, and KNSS compactness [KNSS]; discard pressure/quotient producers. **Disposition.** Retain the proved extraction bridge, not an unproved global rigidity theorem. The attempted maximum-principle and energy-summation closures fail; do not refine those closures this run.

### C. Backward uniqueness with a vanishing singular trace

**Mechanism.** Extract a nonzero blow-up vorticity with zero final trace, then apply backward uniqueness. **Decisive candidate lemma.** A blow-up normalization has a zero final vorticity trace in a topology compatible with the Carleman argument, with its coefficient hypotheses verified. **Logical-strength test.** As currently proposed this packages, rather than proves, the endpoint compactness/tightness information. **Scaling.** Vorticity transforms as `omega_r = r^2 omega`; coefficient assumptions must respect this normalization. **Falsifier.** Test every claimed trace limit against the factor `r^(-1)` in rescaled kinetic energy and against loss of global tails. **Reuse/discard.** The ESS suffix is useful only after its hypotheses are proved; quotient regularity is unnecessary. **Disposition.** Retire the trace-for-free argument. Backward uniqueness is not an a priori producer of its own hypotheses.

### D. Full-vector frequency-cascade rigidity

**Mechanism.** Show that the actual Leray-projected triad dynamics cannot sustain a cascade without a nonsummable physical cost. **Decisive candidate lemma.** A scale-normalized amplification segment forces either return of energy to larger scales or enough additional active volume/branching to make a whole cascade's energy cost nonsummable. **Logical-strength test.** This is a finite-segment dynamical assertion stronger than scalar conservation and not merely an endpoint norm definition; it remains only a candidate. **Scaling.** A single viscous packet costs order `r`, so the proposed branching/volume gain must compensate that factor explicitly. **Falsifier.** Exact interacting divergence-free modes, phase reversal, and the averaged-equation construction of Tao [TAO-AVG]. That averaged construction constrains proofs using only shared estimates, not the actual NS equation. **Reuse/discard.** Reuse energy, Fourier normalization, and the full vector equation; discard cubic quotient/shell calculus. **Disposition.** No decisive lemma proved. Do not count a generic cascade budget as progress.

### E. Geometric-measure structure of the singular set

**Mechanism.** Force a sufficiently large singular set or a nonzero energy atom, contradicting a suitable partial-regularity theorem or finite energy. **Decisive candidate lemma.** One singular point dynamically forces positive one-dimensional parabolic Hausdorff measure of singular points. **Logical-strength test.** Against the known zero-measure conclusion this is already a regularity-producing statement; without an independent propagation mechanism it is a restatement, not a reduction. **Scaling.** Positivity of the relevant Hausdorff measure is invariant as a zero/nonzero property; an energy atom is not supplied by the order-`r` cost. **Falsifier.** Compute the mass of a shrinking packet: it tends to zero, not to a positive atom. **Reuse/discard.** Reuse local energy/suitable-solution tools and scaling; discard quotient calculus. **Disposition.** Retire measure-density-to-atom and singularity-propagation assumptions. The partial-regularity theorem is background, not a source of the missing propagation lemma.

### F. Stochastic-Lagrangian deformation operators

**Mechanism.** In the Constantin–Iyer representation, bound the averaged pullback operator rather than a critical norm of the solution [CI]. **Decisive candidate lemma.** An input-only, finite-horizon `L^3` operator bound for `f -> P E[(grad A)^T f(A)]`, for the inverse stochastic flow of the actual NS drift. **Logical-strength test.** At bare existential finite-horizon quantifiers this also has a continuation-equivalence problem: a regular branch has a bounded deformation operator, and evaluation on the datum recovers velocity control. Genuine additional content would require an explicit estimate from specified inputs, not naming its unknown norm. An estimate through accumulated unknown strain fails the same gate. **Scaling.** The deformation matrix and the `L^3` operator norm are dimensionless under NS scaling. **Falsifier.** Determinant one does not bound singular values; Section 6 shows that exact energy balance and the enstrophy upper inequality do not repair this kinematic inference. **Reuse/discard.** Reuse incompressibility and the exact representation; discard quotient material-response calculus. **Disposition.** Retire the determinant/energy argument. The countermodel does not disprove a specifically stochastic cancellation for actual NS drifts.

### G. Vortex-tube topology and circulation

**Mechanism.** A persistent tube with nonzero circulation and noncollapsing macroscopic length would incur an increasing kinetic-energy cost as its core shrinks. **Decisive candidate lemma.** Every singularity contains a tube retaining both normalized circulation and macroscopic length under viscous evolution. **Logical-strength test.** This is a concrete geometric persistence assertion, not an endpoint norm, but neither persistence property has been derived. **Scaling.** Circulation divided by viscosity is dimensionless; a core-to-length ratio is the relevant geometric parameter. **Falsifier.** The viscous circulation law, rather than an Euler Kelvin theorem, must govern the loop; pathwise material circulation is not simply conserved. **Reuse/discard.** Reuse the vorticity equation and energy; discard pressure/quotient clocks. **Disposition.** Retire topology-only persistence. No tube theorem is imported from inviscid dynamics.

### H. Canonical spectral approximations and a successful certificate index

**Mechanism.** Prove one canonical finite-dimensional approximation satisfies a rigorous global-in-time error certificate. **Decisive candidate lemma.** Some truncation index succeeds for every datum and horizon. **Logical-strength test.** The project's HF28 audit already proves this existential claim equivalent to continuation. It fails the requested non-equivalence gate. **Scaling.** The existing critical stress integral, such as `nu^(-3) integral ||v_N||_6^4`, is dimensionless. **Falsifier.** Recheck whether any bound used to choose the index is uniform in the index and independent of the true solution's critical norm. **Reuse/discard.** Retain the numerical certificate as a conditional verification tool and its local theory; no need for quotient regularity. **Disposition.** Retire as an arbitrary-data primary route unless a genuinely independent success-index theorem is proved.

### I. Renormalized dynamics and a monotonicity principle

**Mechanism.** Put blow-up into a rescaled dynamical system, force a compact omega-limit, and reduce it to a stationary/self-similar object. **Decisive candidate lemma.** Every relevant rescaled trajectory approaches a compact set of stationary profiles by a coercive strict Lyapunov law. **Logical-strength test.** Without a demonstrated coercive law and compact phase space this assumes the conclusion-producing compactness. A new functional's formal derivative is not such a theorem. **Scaling.** Renormalized velocity and time are dimensionless; non-self-similar escape must still be covered. **Falsifier.** The explicit slow-record countermodel in Section 6 leaves the critical scale, and a theorem only about exact self-similarity does not cover that behavior. **Reuse/discard.** Reuse scaling and, only after actual stationary-profile production, the Leray-profile literature [NRS, TSAI]; discard quotient clocks. **Disposition.** Retire the assumed-compact-attractor route.

### J. Vorticity polarization and dimensional reduction

**Mechanism.** Force every nontrivial singular tangent into a genuinely two-dimensional or another rigid symmetry class. **Decisive candidate lemma.** A tangent produced from arbitrary three-dimensional evolution loses one active spatial direction by a dynamically inherited rank constraint. **Logical-strength test.** Such a structural constraint would be different from regularity, but assuming directional coherence or symmetry is not proving it. **Scaling.** Vorticity direction is dilation invariant; rank and symmetry must be inherited by the chosen tangent, not by an unrelated snapshot. **Falsifier.** Generic smooth three-dimensional divergence-free initial fields have no such rank restriction, so a proof must use evolution in an essential way. **Reuse/discard.** Reuse vorticity dynamics, mild compactness, and applicable symmetry-class rigidity; discard quotient calculus. **Disposition.** No structural inheritance lemma proved. Do not turn an existing directional criterion into a claimed arbitrary-data producer.
