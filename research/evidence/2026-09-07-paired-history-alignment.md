## 1. A passive comparison with the actual fine advector

On a finite segment [s,t], define z by

  z_t+nu A z+P B(U,z)=0,       z(s)=v(s).                 (1.1)

This is an auxiliary comparison, not the claimed coarse NS solution. It
stays in the coarse solenoidal space. Because U is solenoidal and P is
self-adjoint, <P B(U,z),z>=0, so

  ||z(t)||2^2+2nu integral_s^t||grad z||2^2=||v(s)||2^2. (1.2)

Its time-dependent coefficients are the actual fine NS field U. Fixed-cutoff
boundedness of B(U,.) gives existence, and (1.2) gives continuation on the
segment. No future critical bound is required to define this finite-cutoff
comparison.

For any pair x,y on this interval define their history distance

  D_s^t(x-y)=||x(t)-y(t)||2^2
                 +2nu integral_s^t||grad(x-y)||2^2.

Let q=Q U. Skewness of B(U,.) gives the exact cross balance

  d<U,z>/dt+2nu<grad U,grad z>=<q,Q B(U,z)>.             (1.3)

Indeed the two nonlinear terms are
-<B(U,U),z>-<P B(U,z),U>
=<U,B(U,z)>-<P U,B(U,z)>=<q,Q B(U,z)>.
Combining (1.2), the energy law for U, and (1.3), gives

  D_s^t(U-z)=||U(s)-v(s)||2^2
                   -2 integral_s^t<q,Q B(U,z)>.        (1.4)

This is a genuine causal signed-work representation of a cross-history
angle. The source is generated by U and z through their shared advector.

## 2. Nonlinear square-root alignment estimate

On the high range Q, A_Q=-Delta is bounded below by N^2. Define

  D_q=nu integral_s^t||grad q||2^2,
  J=nu^(-1) integral_s^t||A_Q^(-1/2)Q B(U,z)||2^2.

Fourier duality and spacetime Cauchy--Schwarz give

  |integral_s^t<q,Q B(U,z)>| <= sqrt(D_q J),

and therefore the concrete alignment lemma

  D_s^t(U-z) <= ||U(s)-v(s)||2^2+2sqrt(D_q J).           (2.1)

Unlike a source-linear propagator estimate, this is a nonlinear square-root
bound joining actual high dissipation with the source's dual drive budget.
It contains neither a strain exponential nor a normal metric condition
number. The budget J must nevertheless be estimated; passivity does not
permit deleting it.

There is a positive input-only upper estimate for J. Let

  M=max(||U(s)||2,||v(s)||2),
  L=nu integral_s^t (||grad U||2^2+||grad z||2^2).

Then

  J <= C N M^2 L/nu^2,                                (2.2)
  D_s^t(U-z) <= ||U(s)-v(s)||2^2
                          +C sqrt(N) M L/nu.           (2.3)

The constant is absolute on R3 and independent of fine radius N_f, horizon,
and all future critical norms. In particular L<=M^2 by the two energy laws.
Equation (2.3) is an additive finite-segment energy-loss estimate, not
Gronwall. For one continuously evolved z, L is additive over time segments.

Proof of (2.2): split U=U_lo+U_hi and z=z_lo+z_hi at radius N/2. The low-low
product has Fourier support at most N, so

  Q B(U,z)=Q B(U_hi,z)+Q B(U_lo,z_hi).

The multiplier A_Q^(-1/2)Q P_Leray div has L2 operator norm bounded by an
absolute constant. Bernstein on z and U_lo, together with their energy
bounds, gives

  ||A_Q^(-1/2)Q B(U,z)||2
    <= C N^(3/2) M (||U_hi||2+||z_hi||2).

Both high factors satisfy ||f_hi||2<=(2/N)||grad f||2. Squaring, integrating,
and multiplying by nu^(-1) gives

  J <= (C N M^2/nu) integral_s^t
                           (||grad U||2^2+||grad z||2^2)
     = C N M^2 L/nu^2.

Also D_q<=L, proving (2.3). The fine velocity need not have radius comparable
to N: its high part was measured in L2, while only U_lo used Bernstein.

The coefficient sqrt(N) M/nu still grows with coarse cutoff. At the desired
critical squared weight N, this estimate gives an N^(3/2) cost times the
energy-loss budget. Across distinct levels these budgets concern different
fine/comparison trajectories, so neither additivity in time nor the separate
energy laws makes their weighted sum telescope. This is the precise failure
of (2.3) to produce the simultaneous critical certificate, not an assertion
that optimal history alignment must have that cutoff dependence.

## 3. Bridge back to the actual nonlinear coarse flow

Let w=U-v and eta=z-v. Subtracting the actual coarse equation from (1.1)
gives EXACTLY

  eta_t+nu A eta+P B(U,eta)=-P B(w,v),   eta(s)=0.        (3.1)

The actual nonlinear advector mismatch remains. It has not been replaced by
an externally prescribed stress. The left transport is skew, so duality and
Young's inequality give

  D_s^t(eta) <= (2/nu) integral_s^t ||w v||2^2.          (3.2)

For example, start from
||eta(t)||2^2/2+nu integral||grad eta||2^2
= integral (w tensor v):grad eta,
absorb half the dissipation, and bound the full history distance by twice
the resulting quantity.

The endpoint-plus-gradient-history triangle inequality therefore supplies
the concrete nonlinear relation

  sqrt(D_s^t(w))
    <= sqrt(||w(s)||2^2+2sqrt(D_q J))
          +sqrt((2/nu) integral_s^t||w v||2^2).          (3.3)

All sources in (3.3) are tied to the actual full system and its same-advector
comparison. This is the surviving relative-entropy style upper estimate.

The first straightforward attempted closure is critical smallness:
spatial Holder and homogeneous Sobolev on R3 yield

  integral||w v||2^2
     <= C (ess sup_{[s,t]}||v||3^2) integral||grad w||2^2.

Thus the last square root in (3.3) is bounded by
C(ess sup||v||3/nu) sqrt(D_s^t(w)). It can be absorbed if this critical ratio
is sufficiently small. There is no arbitrary-amplitude bound or smallness
producer here. Merely making the interval short does not make an arbitrary
L-infinity-time L3 norm small. Replacing that supremum by an averaged energy
quantity would require an unproved correlation between ||v||3 and the
concentration of ||grad w||2^2.

FIRST FAILED IMPLICATION: sharing U as advector aligns U and z at the price
of J, but does not imply alignment of U and the actual v without controlling
the nonlinear work integral integral||v w||2^2 (or its signed precursor).
Neither this term nor the cutoff growth in (2.2) is eliminated by the two
energy laws. No generic strain Gronwall was used to disguise this bridge.

## 4. Arbitrary-amplitude test on actual R3 pairs

The more optimistic premise that dissipation FRACTION alone controls the
history angle is false even when the source is generated by the same fine
advector and all equations are unforced. The counterexample below concerns
an amplitude-uniform premise, not the allowed arbitrary full-input costs.

Fix coarse-supported real solenoidal Schwartz a with

  F0=-Q B(a,a) !=0.

Such data are explicitly available on R3: use narrow smooth Fourier packets
at +/-p,+/-q with p=(1,0,0), q=(0,1,0), polarizations e2 and e3 projected
solenoidally. Choose 1<N<sqrt(2)<N_f with packet-width margins. The output
near p+q is nonzero because
P_Leray(p+q)[(e2.q)e3+(e3.p)e2]=e3. Nonnegative narrow bumps preserve that
component by continuity, and other packet outputs are disjoint there.
The reality condition is supplied by equal real-even packets at opposite
carriers. Every retained interaction is included; no invariant-sector claim
or periodic-to-R3 passage is required.

For R>=1 choose common datum R a, and examine t_R=tau/R. Normalize

  U_R(t)=R y_R(Rt),  z_R(t)=R zeta_R(Rt).

Their equations have viscosity nu/R, the same fixed projections, and common
initial datum a:

  y_tau+(nu/R)A y+B(y,y)=0,
  zeta_tau+(nu/R)A zeta+P B(y,zeta)=0.

Fixed-cutoff energy bounds and smooth ODE dependence make the Taylor
remainders uniform for nu/R in [0,nu]. Initially

  (y_R-zeta_R)(0)=0,
  (y_R-zeta_R)'(0)=F0.

Hence for one sufficiently small fixed tau>0, uniformly for large R,

  ||y_R(tau)-zeta_R(tau)||2^2 >= (tau^2/2)||F0||2^2.    (4.1)

The physical history displacement consequently satisfies

  D_0^(t_R)(U_R-z_R)/(R^2||a||2^2)
      >= tau^2||F0||2^2/(2||a||2^2)>0.                 (4.2)

In contrast its normalized TOTAL dissipative loss obeys

  L_R/(R^2||a||2^2)
    = (nu/R) integral_0^tau
          (||grad y_R||2^2+||grad zeta_R||2^2)/||a||2^2
      ->0.                                            (4.3)

The integral is uniformly bounded because both cutoffs and tau are fixed and
the normalized energy norms stay bounded. This argument requires no theorem
about an infinite-dimensional inviscid limit.

The two history vectors have equal norm R||a||2 exactly. Therefore their
cosine is 1-D/(2R^2||a||2^2), so (4.2) is a genuine nonvanishing angle while
the dissipation fraction tends to zero. The same initial-derivative argument
also applies to the actual coarse NS v_R in place of z_R.

This refutes a bound of the form normalized displacement <= Phi(normalized
dissipation), with one amplitude-independent Phi continuous at zero and
Phi(0)=0, even in the actual same-advector construction. It also refutes an
amplitude-independent linear energy-loss coefficient. The sqrt(N)M/nu
coefficient in (2.3) does grow with R, so the proved estimate survives this
test. No lower bound on its optimal N dependence is asserted, and the test
does not refute a cost allowed to depend arbitrarily on full initial data.
