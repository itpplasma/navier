# HF05: Lamb-vector commutator geometry of pressure work

Status: DISCOVER/REPAIR evidence for smooth unforced Navier--Stokes solutions
on \(\mathbb R^3\).  The note derives an exact commutator identity and a
geometrically sharper estimate involving variation of the speed \(|u|\).
The resulting critical time norm is not controlled by energy, so no
high-pressure or global regularity theorem is claimed.

## 1. Lamb and Bernoulli identities

Let

\[
 \omega=\nabla\times u,qquad L=u\times\omega,qquad
 k={|u|^2\over2},qquad q=p+k.                         \tag{1}
\]

The vector identity

\[
 (u\cdot\nabla)u=\nabla k-u\times\omega=\nabla k-L    \tag{2}
\]

and the pressure relation
\(\nabla p=-(I-\mathbb P)(u\cdot\nabla u)\) give

\[
 \boxed{\nabla q=(I-\mathbb P)L.}                     \tag{3}
\]

Put \(w=|u|u\).  The kinetic part of the Bernoulli pressure contributes zero
globally:

\[
 \int k,u\cdot\nabla|u|
 ={1\over6}\int u\cdot\nabla|u|^3=0.                  \tag{4}
\]

Therefore, using spatial cutoffs and then passing to the limit,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_3
 &=\int p,u\cdot\nabla|u|
   =\int q\,\operatorname{div}w\\
 &=-\langle w,\nabla q\rangle
   =-\langle w,(I-\mathbb P)L\rangle.                 \tag{5}
\end{aligned}
\]

Pointwise \(w\cdot L=|u|u\cdot(u\times\omega)=0\).  Since \(\mathbb P\) is
the self-adjoint orthogonal projector on divergence-free fields,

\[
 \boxed{P_3=\langle\mathbb Pw,L\rangle
 =\langle[\mathbb P,|u|]u,L\rangle.}                  \tag{6}
\]

Here \([\mathbb P,a]b=\mathbb P(ab)-a\mathbb Pb\), and
\(\mathbb Pu=u\).  Equation (6) is not merely the transport cancellation in
different notation: it says pressure work is generated by the failure of the
nonlocal solenoidal projection to commute with multiplication by the speed.

## 2. Increment form and a speed-sensitive estimate

The identity part of \(\mathbb P\) commutes with multiplication.  For the
Calderon--Zygmund part, the commutator has the principal-value increment form

\[
 [\mathbb P,a]b(x)
 =\operatorname{p.v.}\int K(x-y)(a(y)-a(x))b(y)dy,     \tag{7}
\]

up to the fixed matrix signs in the Leray kernel.  Thus only increments of
\(a=|u|\), rather than its absolute size, enter.

Ordinary projector boundedness already gives the estimate needed here:

\[
 \|[\mathbb P,a]b\|_3
 \leq C\|\nabla a\|_2\|b\|_6.                         \tag{8}
\]

Indeed,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \|[\mathbb P,a]b\|_3
 &\leq\|\mathbb P(ab)\|_3+\|a\mathbb Pb\|_3\\
 &\leq C\|a\|_6\|b\|_6
 \leq C\|\nabla a\|_2\|b\|_6.                        \tag{9}
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus (8) does not require a fractional commutator theorem and does not by
itself quantify cancellation from increments.  Formula (7) exposes the
geometry, while (8) is only triangle inequality, projector \(L^p\)
boundedness, Hölder, and Sobolev.

Since

\[
 \|L\|_{3/2}\leq\|u\|_6\|\omega\|_2,
\]

equations (6), (8), Sobolev, and
\(\|\omega\|_2\leq\|\nabla u\|_2\) yield

\[
\boxed{
 |P_3|
 \leq C\|\nabla|u|\|_2\|u\|_6^2\|\omega\|_2
 \leq C\|\nabla|u|\|_2\|\nabla u\|_2^3.}            \tag{10}
\]

The same estimate follows without (6), directly from

\[
 |P_3|\leq\|p\|_3\|u\|_6\|\nabla|u|\|_2,
 \qquad \|p\|_3\leq C\|u\|_6^2.                       \tag{10a}
\]

Consequently the estimate is not a new analytic benefit of the commutator
identity.  Keeping \(\nabla|u|\) instead of immediately replacing it by
\(\nabla u\) nevertheless exposes the depletion ratio

\[
 \delta_{\rm sp}(t)=
 {\|\nabla|u(t)|\|_2\over\|\nabla u(t)\|_2}\in[0,1]  \tag{11}
\]

(set to zero if the denominator vanishes), and becomes

\[
 |P_3(t)|\leq C\delta_{\rm sp}(t)Y(t)^2,qquad
 Y(t)=\|\nabla u(t)\|_2^2.                            \tag{12}
\]

This bookkeeping is sharper when speed varies much less than direction.
It is not a uniformly stronger analytic inequality for arbitrary flows, because
\(\delta_{\rm sp}\) may be order one.

The increment formula (7) would become a new mechanism only if one proved a
strictly smaller scale-sensitive seminorm for \(|u(y)|-|u(x)|\) on the
interactions that also carry the Lamb vector.  Energy gives only
\(\nabla|u|\in L^2_tL^2_x\), with
\(\|\nabla|u|\|_2\leq\|\nabla u\|_2\).  Taking absolute values in (7) and
using only that information returns (8)--(10); it supplies neither a decaying
high-frequency factor nor a sign.  This is the first failed bridge of the
increment approach.

## 3. Correct high-cutoff identity

Let \(Q_J=P_{>J}\) and \(S_J=I-Q_J\) be real-even self-adjoint Fourier
cutoffs.  Starting from \(p=q-k\), not from the full transport cancellation,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 H_J
 &=\int Q_Jp,\operatorname{div}w
  =-\langle Q_Jw,\nabla p\rangle\\
 &=-\langle Q_Jw,(I-\mathbb P)L-\nabla k\rangle\\
 &=-\langle Q_Jw,L\rangle
   +\langle\mathbb P Q_Jw,L\rangle
   +\langle Q_Jw,\nabla k\rangle.                    \tag{13}
\end{aligned}
\]

Using \(\langle w,L\rangle=0\), commutation of \(Q_J\) with \(\mathbb P\),
and \(\mathbb Pw=[\mathbb P,|u|]u+w\), the first two terms combine exactly:

\[
 -\langle Q_Jw,L\rangle+\langle Q_J\mathbb Pw,L\rangle
 =\langle Q_J[\mathbb P,|u|]u,L\rangle.               \tag{14}
\]

Also \(\langle w,\nabla k\rangle=0\) by (4), so the final term is low output:

\[
 \langle Q_Jw,\nabla k\rangle
 =-\langle S_Jw,\nabla k\rangle.                     \tag{15}
\]

Consequently the exact high-pressure formula is

\[
 \boxed{H_J=langle Q_J[\mathbb P,|u|]u,L\rangle
                 -\langle S_J(|u|u),\nabla(|u|^2/2)\rangle.}       \tag{16}
\]

The second term is precisely the correction lost if one incorrectly reuses
the global transport cancellation after applying a high cutoff.

It is energy-controlled at fixed \(J\).  The low-frequency Bernstein kernel
and \(\|w\|_1=\|u\|_2^2\) give

\[
 \|S_Jw\|_\infty\leq C2^{3J}\|u\|_2^2.               \tag{17}
\]

Moreover

\[
 \|\nabla k\|_1\leq\|u\|_2\|\nabla u\|_2.           \tag{18}
\]

Thus, for \(0<\tau<\min\{H,T_*\}\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \left|\int_0^\tau\langle S_Jw,\nabla k\rangle dt\right|
 &\leq C2^{3J}\|u_0\|_2^3
       H^{1/2}\left(\int_0^\tau\|\nabla u\|_2^2dt\right)^{1/2}\\
 &\leq C2^{3J}\|u_0\|_2^4\left({H\over2\nu}\right)^{1/2}.        \tag{19}
\end{aligned}
\]

The correction has the same admissible energy-level form as the audited
low-pressure contribution.

Since \(Q_J\) is bounded on \(L^3\), the first term in (16) satisfies the
same elementary estimate as the full pressure work:

\[
 \left|\langle Q_J[\mathbb P,|u|]u,L\rangle\right|
 \leq C\delta_{\rm sp}(t)Y(t)^2.                      \tag{20}
\]

This bound is independent of \(J\); the high cutoff supplies no small factor.
It can also be obtained directly from
\(\|Q_Jp\|_3\|u\|_6\|\nabla|u|\|_2\).  Thus the high-cutoff commutator
identity has not improved the known absolute norm estimate.

## 4. Exact remaining trajectory norm

Energy gives

\[
 \int_0^H Y(t)dt\leq{\|u_0\|_2^2\over2\nu}.           \tag{21}
\]

Equations (19)--(20) reduce the unbounded part of the high pressure work to

\[
 \boxed{\mathcal G_H(u)=
 \int_0^{\min\{H,T_*\}}\delta_{\rm sp}(t)Y(t)^2dt.}   \tag{22}
\]

The energy estimate controls \(Y\) in \(L^1_t\), not the weighted quadratic
quantity (22).  A sufficient new geometric input would be an input-only
bound on \(\mathcal G_H\), or a signed estimate comparing it to
\(\nu\int D_3\), but neither follows from (21).

One can state a sharper persistence condition: if, on every finite horizon,

\[
 \delta_{\rm sp}(t)Y(t)\leq g(t)quad\hbox{a.e.},
 \qquad g\in L^1(0,H)                                 \tag{23}
\]

with an input-only \(L^1\) bound, then
\(\mathcal G_H\leq\int g(t)Y(t)dt\) is **not** controlled by energy, because
both \(g\) and \(Y\) are only \(L^1\).  The actually sufficient factorization
is instead

\[
 \delta_{\rm sp}(t)Y(t)\leq G_Hquad\hbox{a.e.},       \tag{24}
\]

with an input-only finite \(G_H\), which gives

\[
 \mathcal G_H\leq G_H\int_0^H Y(t)dt
 \leq {G_H\|u_0\|_2^2\over2\nu}.                    \tag{25}
\]

Condition (24) is a concrete speed-direction depletion statement, not a
consequence of energy and not asserted for arbitrary data.

## 5. Scaling and sign tests

Under

\[
 u_\lambda(x,t)=\lambda u(\lambda x,\lambda^2t),
\]

the ratio \(\delta_{\rm sp}\) is invariant and
\(Y_\lambda(t)=\lambda Y(\lambda^2t)\).  Therefore

\[
 \delta_{\rm sp}Y^2dt
\]

is scale invariant.  The missing norm (22) is critical, exactly as required
for a possible high-pressure producer; the commutator has not smuggled in a
subcritical estimate.  Condition (24) is not scale invariant at a fixed
horizon: \(\delta_{\rm sp}Y\) scales by \(\lambda\).  Its bound must transform
accordingly under rescaling, another indication that it is substantial new
input.

Under amplitude reversal \(u\mapsto-u\), the speed \(|u|\) is unchanged,
\([\mathbb P,|u|]u\) changes sign, while
\(L=(-u)\times(-\omega)=u\times\omega\) is unchanged.  Hence (6) changes
sign, consistently with \(P_3[-u]=-P_3[u]\), while \(D_3\) and
\(\delta_{\rm sp}\) are unchanged.  The commutator geometry yields depletion,
not a universal sign.

The compactly supported profiles constructed in `hf02-r3-profile.md` show
that \(P_3\) is not identically zero, so the commutator in (6) represents a
genuine interaction.  The actual-trajectory family in
`hf04-energy-only-spacetime.md` further shows that no bound for (22) uniform
over data can depend only on kinetic energy at fixed cutoff.  These facts do
not refute a complete-datum-dependent estimate along one trajectory.

## 6. Frontier result

**New exact reduction:** full pressure work is the Lamb-vector commutator (6),
and high pressure work is the commutator plus the explicitly energy-controlled
low kinetic correction (16)--(19).

**Speed-sensitive bookkeeping:** equation (10) retains speed variation in
place of one full velocity-gradient factor, but the same bound follows from
the original pressure pairing by (10a).  It identifies a possible geometric
depletion quantity; it is not a new commutator estimate.

**First missing bridge:** energy does not control the critical trajectory norm
\(\mathcal G_H\) in (22).  Neither incompressibility nor the Lamb orthogonality
forces \(\delta_{\rm sp}\) to decay when enstrophy concentrates.

**Repair target:** prove an arbitrary-data, input-only bound for (22), or a
signed comparison of the commutator term in (16) with cubic dissipation.  A
bound that assumes \(L^\infty_tL^3_x\), a higher continuation norm, or
smallness of the datum would be circular or out of scope.

**Non-claims:** the identities and estimate do not prove HF, pressure
absorption, endpoint continuation, or the Clay alternative.
