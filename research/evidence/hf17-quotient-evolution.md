# HF17: pressure-free evolution of the cubic gradient quotient

Status: bounded analytic mechanism, 2026-09-05.

This note derives the heat/Euler evolution of the cubic distance to the
closed gradient subspace and rigorously proves its strain rewrite without
differentiating the nonlinear Hodge minimizer. The remaining high-strain
term is not controlled.

## Quotient functional and minimizer

Let \(\mathcal G_3\) be the closed subspace of \(L^3(\mathbb R^3;\mathbb
R^3)\) generated by gradients, and define
\[
 \mathcal Q(u)=\inf_{q\in\mathcal G_3}{1\over3}\int|u+q|^3dx. \tag{1}
\]
Strict convexity gives a unique minimizer \(q=q[u]\). Put
\[
 w=u+q,\qquad A=|w|w.                                        \tag{2}
\]
Then \(w\in L^3\), \(A\in L^{3/2}\), and first variation in every gradient
direction gives
\[
 \int A\cdot gdx=0\qquad(g\in\mathcal G_3).                   \tag{3}
\]
Equivalently, \(\nabla\cdot A=0\) in distributions. The companion quotient
lemma gives the directional derivative
\[
 D\mathcal Q(u)[h]=\int A\cdot hdx.                           \tag{4}
\]

The Leray projection is bounded on \(L^3\), annihilates
\(\mathcal G_3\), and fixes a solenoidal \(u\). Hence
\[
 u=\mathbb Pw,\qquad q=(I-\mathbb P)w,
\]
so
\[
 \|u\|_3+\|q\|_3\le C\|w\|_3,
 \qquad \|w\|_3^3=3\mathcal Q(u).                            \tag{5}
\]

## Actual Navier--Stokes evolution

Let \(u\) be an actual classical unforced Navier--Stokes solution on a
compact interval \([0,T]\subset[0,T_*)\), with
\[
 u\in C([0,T];H^m),\qquad u_t\in C([0,T];H^{m-2}),\qquad m\ge4.
\]
The chain rule from (4) and
\[
 u_t=\nu\Delta u-(u\cdot\nabla)u-\nabla p
\]
give
\[
 {d\over dt}\mathcal Q(u)
 =\nu\int A\cdot\Delta u dx
  -\int A\cdot((u\cdot\nabla)u)dx.                            \tag{6}
\]
Indeed \(\nabla p\in L^3\cap\mathcal G_3\) on the compact classical
interval, so (3) kills the pressure gradient exactly. All remaining
pairings are finite: \(A\in L^{3/2}\), while
\(u_t,\Delta u,(u\cdot\nabla)u\in L^3\) under the stated regularity.

The heat term is nonpositive. For \(s>0\), the heat semigroup preserves the
closed gradient subspace, and its \(L^3\) contraction gives
\[
 \mathcal Q(e^{s\Delta}u)
 \le {1\over3}\|e^{s\Delta}(u+q)\|_3^3
 \le\mathcal Q(u).                                           \tag{7}
\]
Taking the right derivative at zero using (4) yields
\[
 \int A\cdot\Delta u dx\le0.                                 \tag{8}
\]
This argument needs no smoothness of \(w\) or \(q\).

## Rigorous strain rewrite by inner variation

The identity
\[
 \int A\cdot((u\cdot\nabla)u)dx
 =\int q\cdot((A\cdot\nabla)u)dx                              \tag{9}
\]
cannot safely be proved by writing derivatives of \(w\) or \(q\), since the
minimizer is known only in \(L^3\). Instead use an inner variation.

Freeze the time and let \(\Phi_s\) be the volume-preserving flow generated
by the smooth divergence-free field \(u\). Define
\[
 u_s=u\circ\Phi_{-s},\qquad
 q_s=D\Phi_{-s}^T(q\circ\Phi_{-s}).                            \tag{10}
\]
The second expression is the pullback of a gradient covector and belongs to
\(\mathcal G_3\). For a general element of the closed gradient space, this
follows by approximation with compactly supported smooth gradients;
pullback by the bounded \(C^1\) proper diffeomorphism is bounded on \(L^3\)
and preserves their closure.

Since \(q_s\) is an admissible competitor for \(\mathcal Q(u_s)\), change
variables \(x=\Phi_s(y)\) to obtain
\[
 \mathcal Q(u_s)\le {1\over3}\int
 \left|w(y)+\left(D\Phi_s(y)^{-T}-I\right)q(y)\right|^3dy.    \tag{11}
\]
There is equality at \(s=0\). Moreover
\[
 {u_s-u\over s}\longrightarrow-(u\cdot\nabla)u
 \quad\hbox{in }L^3,
\]
and
\[
 {D\Phi_s^{-T}-I\over s}\longrightarrow-(\nabla u)^T
 \quad\hbox{uniformly}.                                     \tag{12}
\]
The compact-interval Sobolev assumptions make \(u\) bounded with uniformly
continuous bounded gradient, which justifies these limits; \(q\in L^3\)
then supplies domination in (11). Apply the Frechet derivative (4) to the
left side. Comparing the one-sided expansions of (11) for both positive and
negative \(s\) forces equality of their linear coefficients:
\[
 -\int A\cdot((u\cdot\nabla)u)dx
 =-\int A_i(\partial_i u_j)q_jdx.
\]
This is exactly (9), obtained without any derivative of \(q\) or \(w\).
Consequently (6) becomes the pressure-free split
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 {d\over dt}\mathcal Q(u)
 =\nu\int A\cdot\Delta u dx
  -\int q\cdot((A\cdot\nabla)u)dx,
 \qquad \int A\cdot\Delta u\le0.
 \quad}                                                       \tag{13}
\]

## Low-strain coefficient and remaining high strain

Fix an input-selected integer \(L\) and decompose
\[
 v=S_Lu,\qquad u^{hi}=u-v.
\]
The low-strain term in (13) obeys, using (5),
\[
 \begin{split}
 \left|\int q\cdot((A\cdot\nabla)v)dx\right|
 &\le\|\nabla v\|_\infty\|q\|_3\|A\|_{3/2}\\
 &\le C\|\nabla v\|_\infty\|w\|_3^3\\
 &\le C2^{5L/2}E_0^{1/2}\mathcal Q(u).                        \tag{14}
 \end{split}
\]
Here Bernstein and the kinetic-energy inequality give
\(\|\nabla S_Lu\|_\infty\le C2^{5L/2}E_0^{1/2}\).
Thus the low strain is an input-dependent Gronwall coefficient. The only
Euler term not covered by (13) is
\[
 -\int q\cdot((A\cdot\nabla)u^{hi})dx.                        \tag{15}
\]
No energy-level estimate or favorable sign for (15) is proved here. In
particular, bounding it by \(\|\nabla u^{hi}\|_\infty\mathcal Q(u)\) merely
renames the missing high-frequency control.

## Frontier record

**MODE / RESULT:** DISCOVER. The quotient energy has the rigorous
pressure-free evolution (13); its heat part is nonpositive, and its
input-low-strain part has the Gronwall bound (14).

**FIRST GAP:** control the high-strain form (15) by a strict viscous fraction
plus an input-only remainder, or find a different structural cancellation.

**SURVIVING CONDITIONAL SUFFIX:** such a high-strain estimate would combine
with (8) and (14) to produce a critical quotient bound without an explicit
pressure term.

**NON-CLAIMS:** no smoothness of the nonlinear minimizer, high-strain bound,
HF estimate, continuation theorem, or global regularity result is asserted.
